# Unified Version Runtime Matched A/B Experiment

## Purpose

This smoke experiment checks whether the unified version-aware runtime can
reduce stale rollout work without starving the learner. It is a prototype
validation run, not a paper-scale performance result.

The follow-up 12-update run tests the same claim over a longer matched window
after fixing rollout shutdown ordering.

## Setup

- Workload: WebShop, maximum 10 actions per trajectory.
- Model: Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507.
- Hardware: one 8-GPU node; GPUs 0-3 train and GPUs 4-7 generate rollouts.
- Training: 4 updates, 4 consumed trajectories per update, staleness tolerance 2.
- Generation: maximum 96 new tokens per action, seed 46.
- Queue bound: at most 24 outstanding trajectories.
- Checkpoints: disabled, including the final checkpoint.

The three runs use the same workload, model, seed, and training settings:

1. `fifo`: fixed outstanding watermark, FIFO scheduling, no KV rebuilding.
2. `full`: version-adaptive admission, version priority, and post-update KV
   working-set rebuilding.
3. `full_floor`: `full` plus the progress-based supply floor, which prevents
   conservative admission from starving the next learner batch.

## Results

| Runtime | Raw response tok/s | Trainable response tok/s | Trainable traj/s | Learner wait | Stale logical-token fraction | KV block hit |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| FIFO | 271.363 | 148.411 | 0.2887 | 29.50% | 44.58% | 75.75% |
| Full, no floor | 161.378 | 149.980 | 0.2561 | 38.85% | 0.00% | 77.62% |
| Full with floor | 166.977 | 150.701 | 0.2963 | 29.22% | 0.00% | 77.24% |

Compared with FIFO, `full_floor` produced 38.47% fewer raw response tokens per
second, but 1.54% more trainable response tokens per second and 2.62% more
trainable trajectories per second. It eliminated the observed stale logical
token waste and did not increase learner waiting.

The run consumed 16 trajectories. Twelve were generated by the immediately
previous policy version, demonstrating that the runtime salvaged already
invested cross-version trajectories instead of requiring every learner batch
to come from newly admitted work.

## 12-Update Matched Follow-up

The follow-up uses seed 47 and keeps the model, hardware split, batch size,
staleness tolerance, queue bound, and generation limits unchanged. It compares
FIFO directly with the complete runtime including the progress floor. Each run
performs 12 real training updates and consumes 48 trajectories.

| Runtime | Elapsed | Raw response tok/s | Trainable response tok/s | Trainable traj/s | Learner wait | Stale logical-token fraction | KV block hit |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| FIFO | 173.06 s | 297.419 | 128.798 | 0.2774 | 37.76% | 52.18% | 74.73% |
| Full with floor | 150.16 s | 170.741 | 163.216 | 0.3197 | 29.34% | 0.00% | 74.61% |

Compared with FIFO, the complete runtime reduced raw response-token throughput
by 42.59%, while increasing trainable response-token throughput by 26.72% and
trainable trajectory throughput by 15.25%. Learner waiting fell by 22.31%
relative, and the same 12 updates finished 22.90 seconds sooner.

FIFO completed 98 trajectories but trained on only 48. It discarded 46 stale
trajectories after spending 374 inference calls and 1,403,864 logical inference
tokens on them. Of those discarded trajectories, 38 had reached at least four
actions and 32 had reached at least eight actions. The complete runtime
completed 50 trajectories, trained on 48, and recorded no asynchronous stale
discard. Its remaining terminal tail was two in-flight trajectories, versus
four for FIFO.

## Interpretation

The no-floor run is an important negative control: reducing stale work alone
can make admission too conservative and increase learner waiting. The supply
floor closes that loop by protecting learner progress while the admission
controller limits speculative work.

The 12-update result demonstrates the central goodput distinction: FIFO makes
the rollout side substantially busier, but most additional work does not enter
training. Version-aware admission and trajectory priority perform less raw
generation while producing usable data faster.

The KV result is positive but small in this short run: block hit ratio improved
by about 2% over FIFO. The post-update rebuilding cohort generated 32,191
prompt tokens, of which 960 were cached. Longer runs are needed to separate
rebuilding overhead from later working-set reuse.

In the 12-update run, KV block hit ratios were effectively unchanged (74.73%
versus 74.61%). The complete runtime did execute 41 rebuild selections, but the
rebuild cohort exposed 106,034 prefill tokens and achieved only a 0.90% rebuild
hit ratio. The measured end-to-end gain therefore comes from admission and
trajectory progress control; this run does not yet establish a KV benefit.

## Limitations

- Four updates and one seed only establish end-to-end functionality.
- The earlier rollout-loop shutdown timeout was fixed by stopping admission,
  waking environment workers, and shutting down the router before awaiting the
  rollout task. A one-update smoke test and both 12-update runs exited with no
  shutdown timeout.
- WebShop behavior quality is not the target of this system comparison.
- The 12-update result still has one seed and one workload. Multi-seed tests and
  at least one additional AgenticRL workload are required before making a
  general performance claim.
